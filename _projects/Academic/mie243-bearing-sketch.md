<section class="bearing-study__overview" id="the-study" markdown="1">

## A familiar part, taken apart on paper

A bearing looks simple from the outside: one ring turning inside another. These drawings look closer. For MIE243 Dissection #1, I documented the outer race, inner race, retainer, and bearing ball as separate components, using aligned views, centerlines, hidden lines, and dimensions to describe the geometry.

The sheets are dated October 8, 2026 and drawn at a stated scale of 3:1. The dimensions are in millimetres; the enlarged drawing scale does not change the values written on the sheets.

</section>

<section id="reading-the-drawings" markdown="1">

## Reading the mechanism

<div class="bearing-study__components" markdown="1">

<div class="bearing-study__component" markdown="1">

### 01 / Outer race

The outer ring contains the curved track that guides the rolling elements. Concentric circles describe its face; hidden lines and the side profile reveal geometry that cannot be seen from that view alone.

</div>


<div class="bearing-study__component" markdown="1">

### 02 / Inner race

The inner ring surrounds the shaft opening. The stepped side profile distinguishes its shoulders from the raceway, showing why the ring cannot be described by its bore and outside diameter alone.

</div>


<div class="bearing-study__component" markdown="1">

### 03 / Retainer

The cage spaces the rolling elements around the ring. Its repeated pockets are the most visually distinctive feature of the drawing set: a circular pattern in one view, open curved pockets in the other.

</div>


<div class="bearing-study__component" markdown="1">

### 04 / Bearing ball

The simplest component needs the simplest drawing. Centerlines locate the sphere, and a single diameter communicates its size without adding redundant views.

</div>


</div>

<section id="sketch-engineering-significance" markdown="1">

## Engineering significance

A component sketch is more than a record of appearance: it communicates the geometry another engineer needs to understand how parts fit and interact. In this bearing study, the face views establish concentric relationships, while the aligned side views reveal shoulders, steps, and raceway profiles that a single photograph would miss.

The retainer connects individual features to a repeated circular pattern. Documenting its pockets makes the spacing of rolling elements understandable without drawing every feature in every view. The ball drawing shows the opposite case: one diameter is enough to describe a sphere, so additional views would add little information.

Together, the four sheets practise a core engineering skill: translating a physical object into a clear, dimensioned description. They could support a later CAD model or an assembly study, while the explicitly estimated groove radii show where further measurement would be needed before treating the sketches as manufacturing drawings.

</section>
